I used to think you shouldn't show your work until it was polished.

You know the feeling. There's a draft of something — a design, a project, a post — and it's not *quite* there yet. So you wait. You tinker. You tell yourself that when it's ready, you'll share it.

The problem is "ready" is a moving target. There's always one more thing.

## What changed

I started treating my portfolio as a living thing instead of a finished thing. It doesn't have three case studies because I have three finished projects. It has three case studies because those are the ones I've built so far. More will come.

The moment I stopped waiting for "done," the work got better. Not because I suddenly became more talented — but because I started getting feedback sooner. From my mom, who saw the fashion landing page and told me what her customers actually cared about. From a coursemate, who noticed a spacing issue I'd stared past for an hour.

## The uncomfortable part

Building in public means people see the version of you that isn't finished yet. That's the point.

The version of me that ships one project every two weeks will always beat the version of me that's waiting to ship something perfect. Compound interest doesn't care about perfect.
